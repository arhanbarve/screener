### Amendment: both sell limits were re-priced away from the `--auto` cap

Decisions 1 and 2 specified `--auto` with 350bp / 250bp buffers. `--auto` on ORKA returned
a limit of **$87.88** — only **1.61%** below the $89.32 close, not the ~$86.20 a 350bp
buffer off the close implies. Working backwards, the buffer was applied to a reference of
roughly $91.07, which is not a price ORKA traded at near the bell. That is the same class
of stale/bad broker reference that blocked yesterday's ORKA stop at $89.64 against a
phantom $87.935 mark. I did not want the fill probability of a three-day-weekend order set
by a reference I have now seen be wrong twice on the same symbol.

So I cancelled order `6984dff1-…` and re-placed both sells with **explicit** limits about
5% below the actual closing prints: ORKA **$85.00** (4.84% below $89.32), DNTH **$100.50**
(5.04% below $105.84).

This costs less than it looks. A marketable limit in an opening auction fills at the
*auction* price whenever that price clears above the limit — so if Monday opens flat, both
fill near the open and the wider cap is never touched. The cap only binds in a gap-down,
which is exactly the scenario I am insuring against: with the protective stops now
cancelled, an unfilled sell leaves an unprotected position falling all day. Widening is
close to free in the normal case and materially valuable in the tail. The intent of
Decisions 1 and 2 is unchanged — exit both names at a bounded, deliberate price rather
than by market order.
