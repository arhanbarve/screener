## Best call — exiting BFLY on the signal, not on the price

Realizing −$709 does not look like a good call, and that is the point. The screener actively
downgraded BFLY out of the list — not "absent," *withdrawn* — and I sold the whole block at
**$8.281** the next open. **The dust left behind stopped out at $7.642 the same day**, so the
block sale beat the intraday alternative by about **$0.64 a share on 964 shares ≈ $616**.

What makes it the week's best call is the *rule* it validated, which now has a second data
point: **when the screener withdraws the signal that justified a position, sell the position.**
It is also the rule I explicitly declined to apply to VLO, TXG and ETON on the grounds that
mere absence from a twenty-name list is not withdrawal. That distinction held up.

## Worst call — VSXY held into the Q2 print at 7.9% of equity

**−$1,225.45.** Victoria's Secret reported pre-market on 09-03 and closed −13.5%. The floor
at $80.84 filled at **$75.586 — 6.5% below the stop price**, because a gap does not respect a
stop.

The reasoning error is on record from the night before, in my own words: *"on a gap to $75 it
converts a paper loss into a realized one at the worst available price."* I wrote the outcome
and took the bet anyway, on the argument that *"either I believe it or I don't, so halving is
paying cash for comfort."* Two things were wrong:

- **Revision breadth and SUE are estimate evidence, not reaction evidence.** 9-up-0-down
  revisions and an 87% prior-quarter beat say the number will be fine. They say nothing about
  whether a stock up ~300% in a year has already priced it.
- **"Halving is paying for comfort" is only true for symmetric bets.** With a −13% tail one
  calendar day out, halving would have cost ~$613 instead of $1,225 and I would still have
  owned the thesis.

## Lesson of the week

**A stop does not size a position. Only size sizes a position.**

A 7.9% weight into a binary event with a 5% floor is not a 5% risk — it is whatever the gap
turns out to be, and it was 13%. Stops control *drift*; size controls *gaps*. The two failures
this experiment has recorded (VSXY on 09-03, and the 9.6% pre-market-to-fill gap earlier) are
both gap events, and in both the protective machinery worked exactly as designed and still
did not cap the loss.

**Adopted as a standing rule:** no position held into a scheduled print at more than ~4% of
equity. It cost $1,225 to learn and it will not be re-litigated in a session where I happen to
feel confident.
