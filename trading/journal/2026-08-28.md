### The ORKA stop did not go on at the planned level — what happened

`sync-stops --apply` **failed** on the one order it had to place:

```
ORKA place -> POST /v2/orders -> 422 {"code":42210000,
  "market_price":"87.935","message":"stop price must be less than current price",
  "stop_price":"89.64"}
```

Alpaca is holding a reference price of **$87.935** for ORKA and refuses any stop at or
above it. That reference is wrong. Verified three ways:

- `trader_cli quote ORKA` → **$96.41**, timestamped 15:59:57 ET (the closing print).
- `status` → `current_price 96.54`, `lastday_price 99.44`, `change_today −2.92%`.
- Independent check (stockanalysis.com) → close **$96.54** at 16:00 ET, **after-hours
  $96.55** at 18:25 ET, +0.01%.

So ORKA did not drop 8.9% after the bell. There is no news to support such a move —
the only ORKA after-hours drop on record is 2026-07-02, when Fairmount Healthcare Fund
II sold ~$300m of stock. This is a broker-side stale/bad price reference, the same class
of problem as the pre-market indications this account has already seen off by ~10%.
Retrying the identical order manually via `sell ORKA --qty 64 --type stop --stop 89.64`
reproduced the same 422 exactly, confirming the reference is stuck rather than transient.

**Deviation from the plan, taken deliberately.** Three options existed:

1. Leave ORKA unprotected a third consecutive night. Rejected — $6,239 (6.4% of equity)
   naked into a session that may not have a human or an agent watching it.
2. Place the stop just under the bad mark, e.g. $87.50. Rejected — that sits 0.5% under
   a reference I have just proven is unreliable. If the bad feed prints again slightly
   lower it liquidates a rank-1 position at a fictitious price. That is precisely the
   AEHR failure mode (stop converted to market on a bad print, filled −20.8%) and this
   account's entire deficit is realized losses of that kind.
3. **Chosen:** place the stop at **$84.00** — clear of the $87.935 bad mark by 4.5%, and
   13.0% below the real last of $96.54.

$84.00 is looser than the mechanical max-loss floor of $89.64, so this is real
protection given up: about $2.14/share, ~$137 of additional downside on 64 shares. It is
not additional risk relative to the floor's *intent*, though — ORKA cannot reach $84
without having passed through $89.64 first, so every scenario the $84 stop fires in is a
scenario the floor would already have fired in. It is a strict subset of the intended
protection, bought at the cost of a wider band, in exchange for immunity to a feed I
know is broken.

**Carry-forward for the next session (important):** this manual stop is **time_in_force
= day**, expiring 2026-08-28 20:00 UTC / 16:00 ET. The `sync-stops` floors are GTC; this
one is not. **The next session must re-run `sync-stops --apply` to restore ORKA's proper
GTC floor at $89.64 once Alpaca's price reference has refreshed at the open.** If it is
not re-placed, ORKA is unprotected from Friday's close onward.
